## 1. Decision

**Proceed with a staged 14-day pilot, not a full launch commitment.**

The recommended concept is an evidence-led, faceless hybrid channel called:

# **Leonida Field Guide — GTA VI Fan Channel**

**Candidate handle:** `@LeonidaFieldGuideFan`  
**Handle, domain, trademark, and naming availability:** OPEN — live checks required before use.

The channel should operate through five connected editorial desks:

1. **The Briefing** — official news, editions, release information, corrections.
2. **The Evidence Board** — trailers, screenshots, maps, locations, characters, and clearly labeled analysis.
3. **The Field Manual** — searchable guides, settings, routes, progression, collectibles, and updates.
4. **The Field Lab** — controlled gameplay experiments, myth tests, AI/NPC behavior, physics, police, vehicles, and economy.
5. **Leonida Stories** — documentary-style entertainment, challenge narratives, history, and cultural context.

This concept works during active-news periods and quiet-news periods. It does not depend on leaks, rumors, daily filler, copied footage, or one presenter’s face.

### Immediate strategic thesis

The winning position is not “fastest GTA VI news.” That space is crowded and rewards volume, urgency, and speculation. The stronger opening is:

> **The independent GTA VI fan channel that separates what Rockstar confirmed, what official footage shows, what the evidence suggests, what remains a prediction, and what has not been verified—then tests the game rigorously after launch.**

### Done-test for the pilot

Continue only if the first three long videos establish at least one repeatable audience signal:

- search demand;
- strong packaging response;
- strong viewer retention;
- meaningful subscriber conversion;
- or a breakout Shorts-to-long-form path.

- Strength: completeness, trust, clean footage, searchable archive.

- Strategic lesson: build a durable library that remains useful years later.
- Weakness to exploit: limited editorial voice and limited evidence-method differentiation.

- Packaging: one clear promise, recognizable GTA imagery, high-contrast text/object.

- Weakness to exploit: a new channel can be more explicit about source quality, version dates, and testing methodology.

#### MrBossFTW
- Strength: speed, frequency, topic coverage, keyword capture.
- Packaging: urgency, possibility language, frequent updates.
- Strategic lesson: speed matters during news spikes.

- Strength: lean production, consistent GTA Online utility, rapid response.
- Strategic lesson: a small team can win useful search traffic without cinematic production.
- Weakness to exploit: create deeper evergreen assets, stronger visual system, and more systematic experiments.

- Strength: repeatable franchises, challenges, deep game knowledge, clips ecosystem, long-form-to-short-form multiplication.

#### LegacyKillaHD
- Strength: editorial framing, gaming-business context, audience trust.
- Strategic lesson: explain why news matters, not merely what happened.
- Opportunity: a dedicated GTA VI brand can be faster and more comprehensive.

#### ZacCoxTV
- Strength: franchise history, secrets, search topics, accessible narration.
- Strategic lesson: history and comparison content fills quiet-news periods.
- Opportunity: evidence labels and repeatable tests can create a sharper identity.

## 9. Editorial Classification System

Every material claim should be tagged in the research packet and, when useful, on screen.

| Label | Definition | Allowed source |
|---|---|---|
| CONFIRMED | Explicitly stated by Rockstar, Take-Two, or the relevant official platform/store | official first-party source |
| OBSERVED | Visible or audible in official media but not explicitly promised as a system | official trailer, screenshot, page, or approved media |
| ANALYSIS | The channel’s interpretation of confirmed or observed evidence | clearly explained reasoning |
| PREDICTION | A forecast based on stated assumptions | channel analysis; never framed as fact |
| RUMOR | A public claim from a named third party that is not official | named source plus reliability context |
| UNVERIFIED | Claim lacks adequate evidence | may be discussed only to correct or contextualize |
| LEAKED | Unauthorized prerelease material, early-copy footage, hacked files, or leak-derived assets | prohibited from use |

### Headline rule

The title must use the highest-confidence truthful label, not the most clickable possible label.

### Corrections rule

- Material error: correct the description and pinned comment immediately.
- Thumbnail/title error: replace immediately.
- Video-level factual error: add a visible correction card when feasible or publish a correction.
- Guide changed by patch: update title/description with the version/date and either revise or supersede the video.
- Maintain a public corrections page once the website exists.

## 11. YouTube Policy Strategy

### Monetization and inauthentic content

YouTube’s channel monetization rules treat repetitive or mass-produced content as “inauthentic content.” Reused content can fail monetization even when there is no copyright claim or even when permission exists. The channel must make authorship obvious through original research, script, narration, structure, graphics, testing, and editing.

### Reused content operating standard

A video should not be approved internally unless it contains:

1. an original thesis or utility;
2. an original script;
3. persistent original narration or explanation;
4. meaningful editing and visual organization;
5. only the amount of third-party material necessary for the point;
6. source and rights logging;
7. a clear viewer outcome that the source footage alone does not provide.

### Gaming and advertiser suitability

- Ordinary gameplay violence can be monetizable, but violence, blood, or injury should not be the uncontextualized focal point.
- Avoid graphic violence in the opening.
- Avoid graphic or sexualized thumbnails.
- Keep profanity out of thumbnails and titles.
- Do not build montage channels around gratuitous violence.
- Use context—guide, documentary, experiment, criticism, or narrative—rather than gore compilation.

### AI disclosure

YouTube requires disclosure when realistic content is meaningfully generated or altered in a way that could mislead viewers about a real person, event, or place. Production assistance such as outlines, scripts, thumbnail ideation, captions, repair, and ordinary gameplay does not automatically require disclosure. The channel should disclose whenever a reasonable viewer could mistake synthetic material for real footage, an official asset, a real person, or a real event.

### Fan-channel and impersonation posture

- Keep “Fan Channel” in the display name or handle.
- Do not use “Official,” “Real,” “Rockstar,” or deceptive misspellings in a way that implies authorization.
- Do not copy Rockstar’s channel branding, banner, icon, color lockup, or typography.
- Keep the independence disclaimer in the About page and every video description template.

### Copyright posture

- Fair use/fair dealing is jurisdiction-specific and case-by-case.
- There is no universally safe number of seconds.
- A Content ID claim can occur even for short uses.
- A disclaimer, credit, or “no infringement intended” does not create rights.
- Do not automatically dispute claims. Review the exact asset, purpose, amount, and legal basis first.

### YPP path

Current official YouTube thresholds include:

- earlier fan-funding access in eligible regions at 500 subscribers, three public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million valid Shorts views in 90 days;
- advertising revenue eligibility at 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 10 million valid Shorts views in 90 days.

Meeting a threshold does not guarantee acceptance; the channel is reviewed for policy compliance.

## 12. Rockstar and Take-Two Rights Risk

### OFFICIAL SOURCE posture

Rockstar Support states that Take-Two generally does not object to non-commercial fan use that avoids intentional spoilers, while defining “non-commercial” as not making money from the footage and not using it to promote a product or service. It also reserves the right to remove copyrighted material at any time.

Rockstar specifically identifies takedown risk for:

- leaked prerelease footage;
- early unboxings;
- endings and major story reveals;
- edited combinations of cutscenes or plot dialogue;
- isolated cinematics;
- isolated in-game entertainment;
- and material that encourages cheating or illegitimate in-game gains.

### Material business conflict

The proposed channel is designed to monetize. Rockstar’s public policy is framed around tolerated **non-commercial** use. Therefore:

- widespread existing monetized GTA coverage is not proof of a license;
- lack of a claim is not permission;
- YouTube monetization eligibility is separate from Rockstar/Take-Two rights;
- sponsorship makes the commercial character more explicit;
- a channel can be policy-compliant with YouTube and still face a rights claim.

### Recommended rights posture

1. Contact the published Take-Two copyright address with a narrow written description of the planned monetized commentary, guide, and gameplay format.
2. Ask for clarification rather than claiming authorization.
3. Keep the response, if any, in the rights ledger.
4. Obtain legal advice before substantial investment, sponsorship, merchandise, or aggressive dispute activity.
5. Use original gameplay captured from lawfully purchased copies after release.
6. Disable or replace licensed in-game radio/music whenever possible.
7. Avoid isolated story cinematics and entertainment segments.
8. Use prelaunch official images or clips sparingly, transformatively, and only when necessary to explain the analysis.
9. Do not sell downloads containing Rockstar art, screenshots, logos, maps, audio, or characters.
10. Build merchandise around the channel’s original brand—not GTA logos, characters, official art, or imitated trade dress.

**Verification unavailable inside this environment. Human legal validation required.**

## 18. Production Workflow

## A. Research brief

Each video begins with:

- audience;
- search/browse objective;
- one-sentence promise;
- claim classes;
- spoiler level;
- source deadline;
- rights risk;
- visual plan;
- success metric;
- update trigger.

## B. Evidence log

For every material claim:

- exact claim;
- classification;
- source owner;
- source title;
- publication/update date;
- retrieval date;
- exact section or timestamp;
- screenshot or archive reference;
- whether the claim is current;
- whether another source conflicts;
- approved wording.

## C. Rights and asset ledger

For every non-original asset:

- owner;
- source;
- asset type;
- purpose;
- amount used;
- transformation;
- music present;
- license or policy basis;
- claim risk;
- approval decision;
- final timestamps in the edit.

## D. Script

Recommended structure:

1. Cold open: question, stakes, and payoff.
2. Evidence boundary: what sources are allowed.
3. Fast answer.
4. Three to seven evidence chapters.
5. Counterpoint or uncertainty.
6. Decision or viewer action.
7. Next-video bridge.

No generic 20-second logo intro.

## E. Fact check

A second person checks:

- title claim;
- first 30 seconds;
- all “confirmed” statements;
- dates, platforms, editions, prices, and policies;
- rumor labels;
- spoiler level;
- sponsor language;
- description sources.

## F. Voiceover

- Record clean 48 kHz audio.
- Maintain one voice profile.
- Remove filler, repeated clauses, and unsupported certainty.
- Keep energy changes tied to story beats.
- Store project files and release forms.

## G. Gameplay capture

- Capture only from lawfully obtained copies.
- Keep platform, build/version, date, settings, and save state in the log.
- Disable licensed radio/music where feasible.
- Separate clean footage, tests, B-roll, cutscenes, and prohibited entertainment.
- Repeat tests enough to avoid presenting one anomaly as a system.

## H. Edit

- Pattern change every 5–15 seconds when useful, not mechanically.
- Use original diagrams, counters, maps, and evidence cards.
- Keep official clips short and necessary.
- Blur or omit spoilers.
- Keep gore out of the opening and thumbnail.
- Add chapter cards and source labels.

## I. Packaging

Before upload, produce:

- three title candidates;
- two thumbnail concepts;
- one search-first package;
- one browse-first package;
- mobile readability check;
- deception check;
- trademark/impersonation check.

## J. Publish

Description template:

- one-sentence summary;
- independence disclaimer;
- source labels and current-as-of date;
- spoiler level;
- corrections link;
- affiliate/sponsor disclosure;
- chapters;
- related playlist;
- website guide.

Publishing is APPROVAL REQUIRED until Mike establishes a delegated release policy.

## K. Analytics

Check at:

- 1 hour: technical errors and early click response.
- 24 hours: impressions, CTR, first-30-second retention, average view duration, traffic source.
- 72 hours: browse/search split, returning viewers, subscriber conversion.
- 7 days: long-tail search, end-screen flow, comments, corrections.
- 28 days: portfolio role and update decision.

## L. Update

- Revise packaging when impressions are adequate but CTR is weak.
- Revise the opening when CTR is healthy but retention collapses.
- Update the guide when the game changes.
- Build sequels only when viewer demand or performance supports them.
- Do not mass-clone a breakout video with superficial substitutions.

## 24. Success, Failure, Continuation, and Pivot Thresholds

Use metrics only after adequate impressions. A video with very low impressions has not fully tested packaging.

### Minimum continuation threshold

Continue for another 30 days if at least three of these are true:

- 3,000+ combined long-form views in 14 days;
- one long video reaches 1,500+ views;
- average long-form CTR is 4.5% or higher on adequate impressions;
- average first-30-second retention is 60% or higher;
- average percentage viewed is 35% or higher;
- 50+ subscribers;
- one Short reaches 5,000+ views and sends measurable traffic to long form;
- search impressions continue rising after day seven;
- comments show repeat demand for the selected series.

### Target result

- 15,000+ combined long-form views;
- one video above 7,500;
- 6%+ CTR;
- 70%+ first-30-second retention;
- 45%+ average percentage viewed;
- 300+ subscribers;
- meaningful returning-viewer growth;
- at least one clear winning content pillar.

### Breakout result

- 50,000+ combined long-form views;
- one video above 25,000;
- 1,000+ subscribers;
- multiple suggested/browse placements;
- strong demand for a sequel or recurring series.

### Failure threshold

Pause and diagnose if all are true:

- fewer than 1,000 combined long-form views;
- at least 20,000 combined impressions;
- CTR below 3.5%;
- first-30-second retention below 50%;
- no video shows rising search or browse distribution;
- and subscriber conversion is below 0.5%.

### Pivot logic

| Signal | Diagnosis | Action |
|---|---|---|
| high impressions, low CTR | packaging or weak promise | replace thumbnail/title; tighten concept |
| strong CTR, weak first 30 seconds | opening fails the promise | rewrite cold open and pacing |
| strong retention, low impressions | topic or metadata/distribution issue | create adjacent search topics and stronger packaging |
| Shorts perform, long videos do not | weak funnel or long-form premise | create Shorts-native series; improve long payoff |
| buyer guide wins | search utility is the wedge | increase official/buyer/launch-prep coverage |
| map video wins | evidence/world analysis is the wedge | expand Atlas/Evidence Board |
| history video wins | quiet-news evergreen is viable | build franchise-history series |
| all three fail but retention is good | channel has no distribution yet | run three more highly searchable videos before abandoning |
| repeated rights claims | business model risk | stop affected format and obtain legal review |
